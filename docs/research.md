# Aktobe 2000 to 2010: visual reference brief

Research date: 2026-09-25. Purpose: a modelling brief for a stylized walkable Aktobe (Aktyubinsk) neighbourhood set around 2005.

How to read this file:

- Codes like `c07`, `t01`, `r01` point to reference photos. `docs/research-images.md` maps each code to its local file, source URL, date and licence.
- `[S1]` and similar point to the text sources at the end.
- Hex colours are my estimates from the photos. Treat them as a base palette, not as measured values.
- "approx." marks my own estimates from photos. `[unverified]` marks inference or general post-Soviet knowledge that I did not see in an Aktobe source.
- Most dated street photos are from 2007 to 2009. A few 2013 photos show Soviet buildings that did not change. Every photo newer than 2010 is flagged.

## 1. Residential architecture

### Brick and panel blocks (seen)

- **9-storey silicate-brick blocks** dominate the 2007 to 2009 photos (c11, c16, t03). Brick is pale grey-beige, approx. `#D6D0C4`. Loggias run in vertical strips. In c11 the concrete loggia parapets carry a Greek-key relief. Old wooden frames and new white PVC frames mix on one facade.
- **Kazakh ornament in red brick** `#A8452E` decorates the top floors or blind end walls of several 9-storey brick blocks (c16, c50). c08 shows a red-brick head and a diagonal stair motif on a silicate end wall.
- **9-storey panel blocks** with a small beige ceramic tile finish `#D9CBB0` and diamond accent tiles near the roof (c37). Balcony fronts alternate pastel yellow `#EFE2A0` and light blue `#A9CBE3` in vertical bands.
- **5-storey Khrushchyovka blocks**: cream brick or panel blocks with flat roofs (c50, 2013). My sources do not name the Aktobe series. Soviet series 1-447 (brick) and 1-464 (panel) are the likely types `[unverified]`. Typical size: approx. 15 m to roof, 12 m deep, 60 to 100 m long with 4 to 6 entrances, 2.8 m floor to floor `[unverified]`.
- **2000s facade clutter** (c07, c11, c37): split AC units under windows, satellite dishes, balconies enclosed in plastic siding or sheet metal (blue `#2F5FA8` in c07), steel grilles on ground-floor windows (c14), TV aerials on the roof (c11).
- **Plinths** of older buildings are often painted strong red `#B8342B` to about 1 m (c14).

### Stalin-era and early post-war buildings (seen)

- 2 to 4 storeys, pitched metal roofs, stucco in ochre `#E3B878` (c07, c47) or pale yellow `#EDE0A6` (c43, c46, 2013 photos). White trim `#F2F0EA`: cornices, window surrounds, balusters, a pediment with a round window (c47).
- Arched gateways into the courtyard (c46). Dark red-brown plinth.
- c07 (Dec 2009) shows a yellow gas pipe along the facade at first-floor level.

### New buildings of the 2000s boom (seen)

- 10 to 12 storey beige brick towers with red metal-tile hipped roofs and glazed bay windows (c33, uploaded 2008).
- Glass boxes: teal mirror glass on a red brick base for the bowling club (c02, c20), blue mirror glass with a blue glass dome (c34). Use them only in a late-decade scene.

### Entrances and courtyards

- c12 shows an office entrance in a residential block: white PVC door with a glass upper panel, brown steel outer door, corrugated metal canopy ceiling, bilingual hours plate "ЖҰМЫС УАҚЫТЫ / РЕГЛАМЕНТ РАБОТЫ".
- Courtyards in c11 and c50: bare earth, patchy grass, parked cars, black steel bar fences, elms and poplars.
- Not in my Aktobe photos, but standard in every Soviet city: concrete podyezd canopies, benches by the door, steel garages, laundry poles, carpet-beating frames, metal climbing frames and swings `[unverified]`.
- New 2000s playgrounds are bright plastic (c18, Dec 2009): purple and green dome roofs, red and blue tube slides, beige play houses.

### Private sector (seen in c23, c24, June 2008)

- Single-storey houses with gable roofs. Walls are white lime plaster with blue window frames `#3A6FB0`, or wood cladding in maroon `#6E2A2E` with a light blue gable `#9CB8D8`.
- Roofs of seamed silver sheet metal `#A8AAA8`, green sheet metal `#4E7A5A`, some blue.
- Shutters, white-painted window bars, a small attic window in the gable.
- Solid sheet-metal gates in maroon or blue with a wrought-iron arch over the gate (c23). Grey wooden plank fences (c24).
- Streets are dirt or a narrow asphalt strip with wide sandy shoulders `#C8B08A`. Wooden power poles carry a web of wires. In 2013 only 266 of 621 km of city roads had asphalt [S7].

## 3. Streets and infrastructure

- **Main avenue** (c01, 2007): 3 lanes each way, a wide green median with young trees, tall lamp posts and billboards. Wikipedia gives 5.5 km, 4 lanes normally and 6 near the akimat, and a tree-lined median [S3].
- **Markings**: white dashed lines and zebra crossings (c32, c50). Asphalt `#5A5A5C`, pale and dusty at the edges `#7A746C`.
- **Kerbs**: plain grey concrete (c13, c32). A black and white striped base on a traffic-light pole in c15.
- **Trolleybus: yes.** It ran from 1982 to 2013 [S1][S8]. In the 2000s only route 1 remained, Studencheskaya stop to Pushkin Park [S1], as on the boards in t01 and t02. Routes 4, 5 and 5a closed in 2000 [S1]. Wires hang over Abilkayyr Khan Ave in c15, c21 and c38 (Dec 2009), on concrete poles and steel lattice masts (t02). **No tram** ever ran in Aktobe: neither [S1] nor [S8] lists one.
- **Traffic lights** hang from white steel truss gantries across the road (c15, c34).
- **Street lamps**: concrete poles with one cobra-head arm (c13), white steel poles with double arms (c15), globe lamps on black posts in squares (c02, c18, c25).
- **Bus stops**: a long pavilion with a blue corrugated roof (c50, 2013). A kiosk pavilion with a red roof and red dome, green walls and barred windows (c06, 2009).
- **Utilities**: many overhead wires on wooden or concrete poles in the private sector (c24). Yellow gas pipes on facades (c07). Above-ground heating mains with silver cladding are typical but not in my photos `[unverified]`.
- **Fences**: black wrought iron with hearts and scrolls (c18, c20), a low black loop fence round lawns (c46, c50), grey or white steel pedestrian rails (t03).
- **Trees**: small-leaf elm (karagach) and poplar dominate `[unverified species ID from photos]`. Blue spruce and pine in formal spots (c16, c25, c26). Almost every trunk is whitewashed to approx. 1.2 m (c13, c14, c16, c18). Some elms are clipped into balls (c18, c38).
- **Water**: the Ilek flows in a wide floodplain beyond the old town [S15]. The Kargala reservoir is a local landmark.
- **Ground and horizon**: sandy beige dirt at road edges and in the private sector (c23, c24). A flat steppe horizon at the city edge (c24, c42).
- **Climate and light** [S16]: January mean -12.5 C, July mean +23.0 C, 317 mm precipitation per year, snow cover about 136 days, mostly partly cloudy. Season options: summer with a hazy pale blue sky `#8DB6E6` and cumulus (c01, c28), clear frosty winter with a deep blue sky `#7FA8DC` and hoarfrost (c13, c16), grey overcast winter `#D8D8D4` (c06). Autumn sunsets turn the altocumulus pink and violet (c36).

## 4. Transport 2000 to 2010

### Trolleybuses (fleet data from [S8])

- **ZiU-682V** (1982 and 1988 builds) and **ZiU-682G** (1993 to 1994 builds, all withdrawn in 2007). Livery in t02 (Jan 2007): cream-white `#EDE9DA` with two red stripes `#C8282E`, red fleet number, bilingual route board. Size approx. 11.8 x 2.5 m `[unverified]`.
- **BTZ-5276-04**: 4 new units in 2006, 6 more from Karaganda in 2010. Livery in t01 and t03 (Apr 2008): white body, deep blue skirt `#1E56C8`, blue front band with the BTZ logo, red fleet number, digital destination board in Kazakh.

### Buses and marshrutkas

- Withdrawn models per Wikipedia [S9]: Ikarus 260, MAN NL202 and SL202, LiAZ-677, LAZ-695, Hyundai County and Aero Town, PAZ-4230 "Avrora", Setra S215SL, GAZelle marshrutkas.
- The fotobus retired list [S17] adds Ikarus 256, 266 and 280, LiAZ-5256, Mercedes O405, Karosa LC735, Hyundai AeroCity 540, Daewoo BS106, PAZ-3205, PAZ-672M, KAvZ-3976, RAF-2203, GAZ-3221, Mercedes T1 and Sprinter 312D, Ford Transit, and small Chinese buses. Service dates are behind a login, so the exact 2005 mix is `[unverified]`.
- A plausible 2005 mix `[unverified]`: GAZelle and older Mercedes or Ford vans as marshrutkas, PAZ-3205 and used European city buses (MAN SL202, Ikarus 260 and 280) on main routes, some Korean midibuses.
- Seen: a white Hyundai-type midi coach with yellow side stripes (c07, 2009), a white van-based minibus behind the trolleybus (t01, 2008), the FC Aktobe Setra S215HD coach (c35, 2007).
- A 2013 blog describes marshrutka conductors, often women, who collect fares and help people in and out [S15]. The fare was 35 tenge in 2011 [S9]. I found no 2005 fare.

### Cars (seen in 2007 to 2009 photos)

Lada 2107 in white and blue (c23, t01), Lada 2109 and 2110 (c25), Opel Vectra A (c07, c15, c25), VW Passat B3 sedan and a red B3 estate taxi with a roof sign (c15, c41), Toyota Camry XV30 (c07, c15), Toyota Land Cruiser 80 and Prado 120 (c07, c15), Nissan Terrano II (c15), Mitsubishi Outlander (c07), BMW E34 (c29), GAZelle vans and a GAZ-3307 truck (c24, c38). A recent fleet study puts LADA at 26.7 percent, Audi at 8.7 and VW at 8.3, and says over a million used cars came from Germany in the first decade of independence [S18]. So also add Audi 80 and 100, Mercedes W124, VW Golf and Volga GAZ-24 or 3110 `[unverified for Aktobe]`.

### Licence plates (seen)

- 1993 to 2012 format: black characters on white, no flag, no "KZ". Region letter, 3 digits, then 3 letters for private owners or 2 for companies [S19].
- Aktobe region letter is **D** [S19]. Plates I read: "D 362 UDM", "D 625 MDM", "D 523 TVM" (c07), "D 201 KCM" (t01), "D 571 PHM" (c23). Every readable private plate ends in M. The rule behind that is `[unverified]`.
- The flag, "KZ" and region number "04" came in 2012 [S19]. Do not use them for 2005.

### Trains

- The lines through Aktobe had **no electrification** in the 2000s. Electrification of Kandyagash to Tobol and Tobol to Nikeltau is a 2020s project [S20]. Use diesel only.
- Depot TChE-2 Aktobe ran **2TE10M, 2TE10U and 2TE10V** twin-section diesels built in the 1980s [S21]. TE33A diesels are in today's fleet [S21] and came at the very end of the decade `[unverified]`.
- Liveries at the depot in Jan 2007: Soviet dark green `#2F6B3A` with red `#C0302A` and yellow bands (r01), and the new KTZ scheme with a light blue `#56A6DA` body, red front, yellow chevron and blue skirt (r02).
- Blue-grey passenger coaches with a pale stripe (r01), dark green or blue coaches at the platform in 2005 (c28), red-brown freight gondolas (r01). A steel footbridge with an arched truss crosses the station tracks (c28).

## 5. Street life, props and signs

- **Kiosks**: small metal pavilions with sloped metal roofs and barred windows (c06), a small roadside kiosk with a yellow roof (c34). Goods such as cigarettes, drinks, snacks and phone top-up cards `[unverified]`.
- **Shop shelves** in Dec 2009 (c19): Tide, Persil, Ariel, Миф and Losk with hand-written tenge price tags, 115 to 390 tenge per box.
- **Payphones**: Kazakhtelecom card payphones, models URMET 1530 and TMGS 15280, cards of 25 to 1000 units [S22]. Booth look `[unverified]`.
- **Mobile operators** [S23][S24]: K'Cell (Feb 1999) and its prepaid brand Activ (Sept 1999). K-Mobile (Feb 1999) and its prepaid brand Excess (Dec 2002). Beeline from 26 April 2005. All Kar-Tel brands became Beeline on 1 July 2007. Kcell restyled its brand in 2009. Dalacom and PAThWORD (2004) were CDMA brands of Altel. c21 (Dec 2009) shows the yellow and black striped Beeline ball on a 9-storey roof. Pre-2009 Kcell and Activ logo colours `[unverified]`.
- **Patriotic banners**: a billboard "Жаса, жайна Қазақстан!" with the golden warrior on a snow leopard from the Independence Monument (c15), a blue banner "Гүлдене бер, сүйікті Отаным-Қазақстан!" (c37), New Year banners "Жаңа жылыңызбен! / С Новым годом!" (c41). "Kazakhstan-2030" boards are likely but not in my photos `[unverified]`.
- **Sign language**: Kazakh in Cyrillic plus Russian, often on one sign (c12, c26, c27, c31, t02). Examples: "Сүт тағамдары / Молочная продукция" (c31), "АВТОТҰРАҚ / АВТОСТОЯНКА" (c20). Use "Азық-түлік / Продукты" and "Дәріхана / Аптека" the same way `[unverified wording]`. No Latin-script Kazakh and no QR codes. The QR house plates in c55 date from 2016 or later [S7].
- **Sign styles**: big 3D rooftop letters ("ЛОКОМОТИВ" c29, "АКТОБЕ" c26), bold yellow sans-serif on a blue fascia (c31), coloured plastic letters on shopfronts (c37).
- **Exchange rates**: USD/KZT about 142 in 2000, about 153 in 2002, 132 to 146 until 2005, about 120 in 2006 to 2008, about 150 after the 4 Feb 2009 devaluation [S25][S26]. A 2005 board can show about 130 `[unverified exact]`.
- **Flag**: sky blue field, gold sun, gold steppe eagle, gold ornament stripe at the hoist. It flies on the akimat, on rows of poles (c21) and over the bazaar gate (c31).
- **Internet cafes and computer clubs**: not in my sources `[unverified]`.

## 10 details that say "Aktobe, 2005"

1. A 9-storey silicate-brick block with a red-brick Kazakh ornament on the end wall.
2. Whitewashed tree trunks along every street, some elms clipped into balls.
3. Trolleybus wires over Abilkayyr Khan Ave and a single cream ZiU with red stripes on route 1 (a white and blue BTZ after 2006).
4. White plates like "D 362 UDM", no flag.
5. The turquoise domes and 57 m minaret of Nurdaulet by its shopping centre. No gold Nur Gasyr dome before autumn 2008.
6. The pink bazaar arch with "ОРТАЛЫҚ БАЗАР" in yellow on a blue fascia.
7. Opel Vectra A, Passat B3, Audi 80, Lada 2107 and a GAZelle marshrutka in one queue.
8. Loggias glazed in mismatched frames, with split AC units and satellite dishes.
9. The white and light blue station with lancet niches, and a green 2TE10M diesel at the platform.
10. Sandy road shoulders, yellow gas pipes and a flat steppe horizon at the end of the street.
